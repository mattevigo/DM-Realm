# Import an Adventure

Bring one or more Parts of an official Adventure from the Trusted Source into its folder in Adventures, translated into the Workspace Language. The rules — the Adventure's folders, a note per book heading and the Part note, unique names, section ids, links to what is not imported yet, the README, the Campaign READMEs' links and the one yes for the Part's monsters and items — are the `dmr-workspace` skill's structure.md, in its Adventures section ("Importing an Adventure", "The README"); this skill is the procedure that applies them. Speak to the DM in the Workspace Language.

Two helpers do the mechanical work. The renderer is `dmr-import`'s, next to that skill: `<dmr-import>/render-adventure.py`, the path of this skill's base directory with `dmr-import-adventure` replaced by `dmr-import`; run it as

```sh
DMR_SOURCE_CACHE="${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_DATA}/source-cache" python3 "<dmr-import>/render-adventure.py" <adventure id> [--part <index> …] --edition <Edition in use>
```

and read its docstring (the top of the file) once: its modes, the plan and links formats, its exits. The writer, `adventure-notes.py` next to this file, is run from the Workspace root. Never write an Adventure's text from memory or retype what a helper printed.

1. **Load the rules and the data.** Invoke `dmr-workspace` (read its structure.md in full), then `dmr-trusted-source`. Find the Workspace Language and the Edition in use as structure.md's "Fixed after Setup" reads them. Make a scratch folder outside the Workspace (`mktemp -d`) for the plan, the links and the renders; nothing of them goes in the Workspace.
2. **Find the Adventure and its Parts.**
   1. The Adventure: its English title from the Translation Glossary's row for the DM's words, or your translation of them, matched against `data/adventures.json`; the renderer with only its id lists its Parts (index, name, section id, ordinal).
   2. The Parts: one named by its name, or by its ordinal ("chapter 2" is the Part whose ordinal is chapter 2, "appendix B" likewise); "the whole Adventure" is every Part; "the next part" is the first Part in book order not imported yet. A name that matches several Adventures or Parts, or none, is asked about in step 4.
   3. The Adventure's folder and subfolders, by their names in use (the Translation Glossary), or translated like any default folder name.
   4. A Part whose Part note exists and is not empty is already imported: report it and skip it. Find its note by its section id, not its path: Grep the Adventure folder for the id at the end of a source property (`#b00`). A Part with notes written but no Part note is a stopped Import that this one resumes; its written notes are found the same way and kept.
3. **List each Part**: the renderer's `--part <index> --list`. It gives the Part's words, its images (a `size` of null is an image not cached yet, fetched when the Part renders), its headings, and its link targets. A monster or item target is missing when no note, or only an empty one, is at the path step 5 gives it.
4. **Ask once, then stop** — when something needs the DM. In one message: the Parts to import, with their total words and images (count, and size where known); how many of the monsters and items they name the Workspace lacks, asking whether to import those too (on a no their links are written all the same); and every ambiguity from step 2. Write nothing before the answer. Importing several Parts or the whole Adventure always asks. One Part asks nothing when the request already answers — it said whether to bring the missing monsters and items, or none is missing: give the totals and go on.
5. **Plan each Part**, from its `--list`:
   1. **Which headings get a note**, and its folder, as structure.md's "Importing an Adventure" says. Only `entries` and `section` headings can be planned. A heading that only groups others ("Areas of the Stair", "Special Events") stays in the Part note. An item heading the Trusted Source has as an entry — a `links` target in `adventures.<adventure>.items` by that name — gets no note of the plan: it is imported as that entry in step 9, and its heading stays in the Part note. Every other item heading is marked `"item": true`.
   2. **Names.** Each note is named after its heading, translated as structure.md's "Translating a term" says — the Glossary first; record each translation you choose in the Glossary now, as the links need it — with the name rules applied, and unique in the Adventure across its subfolders and its notes already written, a clash qualified as structure.md says. The Part note is named after the Part as the book titles it (the renderer's Part name). A note already written (step 2.4) keeps its path.
   3. **Links**: every `--list` target key mapped to its note's path from the Workspace root, without `.md`. A monster or an item gets the path `dmr-import` would give it (its folder from the target's `folder`, its name translated through the Glossary, recorded now); an item of this Adventure, its path in this Adventure's Items; a `part|<index>`, that Part's Part note path, named as in 5.2 — so each link works when its target arrives (ADR 0013).
   4. Write the plan and the links as JSON files in the scratch folder. The plan is not shown to the DM for approval; it is in the report.
6. **Render the whole Part first, writing nothing**: `--part <index> --plan <plan> --links <links> --check`. On exit 2, correct what it names and check again. On 6 or 7, that Part cannot be imported yet: write nothing for it, name its section and why (from the message), and go on with the others. On 3, the Trusted Source is unreachable: stop. Then render it for real, which fetches its images, into the scratch folder: the same command with `--attachments "<the Adventure's Attachments folder>"` in place of `--check`, its output in `<scratch>/part-<index>.json`. Then split it:

   ```sh
   python3 "<this skill's base directory>/adventure-notes.py" split "<scratch>/part-<index>.json" "<scratch>/en-<index>" --source-key "<the source property's key in the Workspace Language>"
   ```

   It prints each note, in the order to write them, with its English text file, its `path`, its frontmatter and whether it is `done` (already written).
7. **Place the images**, before any note embeds them: `python3 "<this skill's base directory>/adventure-notes.py" images "<scratch>/part-<index>.json" --attachments "<the Adventure's Attachments folder>"`.
8. **Write the notes**, every note not `done`, the Part note last, after every other note of its Part:
   - **An English Workspace**: `python3 "<this skill's base directory>/adventure-notes.py" write "<scratch>/part-<index>.json" --source-key source` writes them, the rendered text as it is.
   - **A translated Workspace**: first **one pass over the Part's English text** collects every name and game term in it not in the Glossary yet — headings, places, NPCs, monsters, items, spells, conditions, rules terms — and records each translation you choose in the Glossary, as "Translating a term" says. You are the only writer of the Glossary. Then each note is translated and written by these instructions: translate the English file into the Workspace Language faithfully — every paragraph, list, table and number, nothing added or left out (structure.md's "Imported notes"); every name and term as the Glossary gives it, a fallback term with its English original at its first occurrence in the note; each link's target, each embed (`![[…]]`) and the frontmatter exactly as given, only a link's text (after its `|`) and an image's caption translated; write the note at its `path`, the frontmatter, a blank line, then the translated text. A Part of up to about 1,000 words you translate yourself, note by note. A longer one goes to **one subagent per note** (the Agent or Task tool; several at once), each given the note's English file, `path` and `frontmatter`, the Glossary's path and the instructions above, plus: a name or term it needs that the Glossary lacks makes it write nothing and return those terms; record their translations and run that note's subagent again.
9. **The README**, as structure.md's "The README" says: written now if the Adventure has none, from `data/adventures.json`, and the Part linked from its line in the Parts list. A run stopped midway resumes from step 2.4, skipping the notes already written.
10. **The monsters and items the DM accepted** in step 4 (and an item heading planned out in 5.1): invoke `dmr-import` for them, as one batch.
11. **On the Adventure's first Import** — no README before this one — link the plain mentions of it in Campaign READMEs, as structure.md's "The README" says, keeping the DM's words as the link text; a mention that might be another Adventure stays as it is.
12. **Report**, Part by Part: its notes, by folder (the plan), and the images placed; the monsters and items imported, and how many links wait for a target not imported yet; the README written or updated; each Campaign README linked to it, and each mention left as it was; and anything not written, with why — already imported, cannot be imported yet (its section), unreachable, or the question you asked.

Done when every Part the DM asked for is written, reported, or waiting on the one question you asked — and nothing else in the Workspace changed but the Glossary rows, the README, what `dmr-import` brought in on the DM's yes, and, on the Adventure's first Import, the Campaign READMEs that mention it.
