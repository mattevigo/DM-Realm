> /dm-realm:dmr-adventure <the Adventure, and what to design in it>

Ask it like this

  • let's design an adventure called The Drowned Bell
  • The Drowned Bell has three parts: the harbour, the sunken temple, the bell tower
  • give me three hooks for The Drowned Bell
  • draft the rooms of the sunken temple
  • review my adventure

How

An Adventure of your own lives in Adventures, beside the official ones, and knows no table: a Campaign runs it, whole or in part, as it runs a published one, and what happens at the table stays the Campaign's. You design it in four stages, in any order, as often as you like:

  1. Premise: the README — pitch, level range, length, world, tone, hook.
  2. Structure: its Parts, each a note with its summary, its scenes or nodes, the clues the party must find and the ways out.
  3. Content: a Part's places, NPCs, encounters and items, each a note of its own. Each encounter's difficulty comes from your Edition's Dungeon Master's Guide, with the page. A monster you invent goes into Homebrew, so any Campaign can use it.
  4. Review: a report, in the chat, of what the Adventure lacks — a conclusion with fewer than three clues, a Part with no way out, an encounter too hard for its levels, a link that leads nowhere — each with the practice or rule it comes from.

Ask for ideas and Claude offers two or three, checked against your Adventure, its world and the rules; ask for a draft and it shows you every note first. Either way, only what you keep is written.

Never

  • Changes an official Adventure: a change you make to one belongs to the Campaign that runs it.
  • Writes an idea or a draft before your yes, or fills a section on its own.
  • Writes official text from memory: the DMG's guidance and its encounter numbers are read and cited.
  • Links your Adventure to a Campaign or a Character, or makes a map or an image: it places the files you give it.
  • Changes anything when it reviews.
