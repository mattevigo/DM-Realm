The Player Wiki is a copy of the parts of your Workspace your players may read, written into a folder outside the Workspace. dmr-player-wiki writes it, and writes it again whenever you ask, typically after a new Chapter.

  • make a wiki for my players
  • update the Player Wiki
  • leave the Characters out of the wiki

What goes in

You choose whole parts. The first time, Claude proposes all of them and asks where the folder should go.

  • Reference, Characters and each Campaign's Chronicle, as they are.
  • A Character Sheet for each Character: a page in the style of a D&D character sheet, made from its stat block and linked from its note, for its player to open on a phone or a laptop. It lists every skill with the ability it uses, the proficient ones marked. It shows maximums (hit points, spell slots), never what was spent at the table, and it is written again at every update, so it always matches the Character.
  • Homebrew and World, minus their Secrets and DM Notes sections and their %% comments %%. Keep secrets under another heading? Tell Claude it is a DM heading too.

Never Adventures, Sessions, a Campaign's other notes, the Voice, Past Builds, DM Tools or the Templates. A link to a note left out becomes plain text, so the wiki has no broken links.

A copy, written by a script

The copying is a script, not Claude, so nothing is reworded, and your Workspace is never changed. The folder is rewritten from scratch at every update: a note you delete leaves the wiki too, and an edit made in the wiki is lost. After each update you get a report of what went in and of every section removed, note by note. Look it over before you share.

As a website

Say yes to the website when Setup asks, or run Setup again later. Then every update also builds the wiki with Quartz into a folder of HTML pages. It is in your Workspace's language, with search, backlinks and a graph.

  • Node 22 or later is needed. Setup checks for it and tells you how to install it. Without it, the Markdown wiki is still written.
  • The first build downloads Quartz, once for every Workspace. DM Realm keeps the version it was tested with.
  • Nothing on the site comes from another server: no analytics, no fonts or scripts from a CDN.
  • A build that fails leaves your previous site as it was.

Look at it before you share it. The report gives you the command, python3 -m http.server --directory "<site folder>" 8080, then you open http://localhost:8080.

Publishing is yours

Upload the site folder to any static host you like. DM Realm does not publish it and does not control who reads it. Reference holds official text, so where you put the site and who can open it is your responsibility. A site only your players can log in to is the safe choice.

Without a website

The Markdown folder is plain Obsidian Markdown: your players can open it as a vault in Obsidian.
